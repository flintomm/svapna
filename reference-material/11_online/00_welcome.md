# Orientation

*Svapna is a source-led study of the history, philosophy, textual traditions, and science around dreaming.*

---

## Scope

The lessons examine dream yoga, lucid dreaming, and accounts of consciousness through texts, historical scholarship, and scientific research. Guided practice, clinical care, and sleep treatment are outside this scope.

## Structure

The curriculum contains 47 lessons in six modules, plus this introduction.

**Modules 1–4: History and traditions.** Historical texts and philosophical accounts of dreaming, including the development of dream yoga. Each tradition has its own terms, purposes, and claims; similarities do not establish equivalence.

**Module 5: Laboratory and clinical research.** Scientific studies of dreaming and lucidity, including clinical applications and limits of the evidence.

**Module 6: Contemporary points of contact.** Encounters between contemplative traditions, philosophy, and dream research.

## Terms and sources

Original terms appear in transliteration with English explanations. Diacritics distinguish words and sounds in languages such as Sanskrit and Pāli. The glossary collects terms for reference.

Quoted translations retain the translator’s wording, with the translator named and citations attached to the relevant passages. Textual teachings belong to their named sources. Editorial interpretation, historical claims, and empirical evidence remain distinct kinds of account; a teaching about consciousness is not itself a scientific finding.

## Reading sequence

The numbered sequence supplies context and vocabulary for later lessons. Individual lessons are also available through the curriculum index; the glossary and library provide reference material.

The first lesson, **Why Begin Here**, introduces the questions about dreaming that frame Module 1.
